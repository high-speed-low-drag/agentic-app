## What to find
Design 2–3 cases. Each tells one planning story that the app's views show well: the voltage envelope crossing the
limits, the loading heatmap going over 100 %, violation episodes on specific days and times, the network snapshot
changing colour through the day, and the clock showing day vs night. Suggested stories; improve or replace them if
you find better ones:
1. **Vision MV, summer PV overvoltage and reverse flow.**
   - Rooftop PV on the loads in **add** mode with a negative magnitude, so midday overvoltage appears above 1.05 pu,
     with reverse-flow loading on transformers or cables.
   - Add a residential evening load so the day/night contrast is visible.
   - Use only realistic, defensible adjustments. If the network needs to be less stiff, use per-case overrides with a
     plausible justification, e.g. a realistic source short-circuit power, or a source `u_ref` around 1.02–1.03 as
     substations often run. Avoid absurd numbers. The power flow must converge on every timestep (check
     `n_failed_timesteps` = 0).
2. **Vision MV, winter evening peak with EV and heat-pump-like growth.**
   - Undervoltage below 0.95 and overloading above 100 % on specific feeders or LS transformers in the evening, using
     residential + EV add + a load growth factor.
3. **IEEE 14, a rated transmission study.**
   - Give the generic branches plausible MVA ratings as overrides. Base them on the typical IEEE 14 flows, e.g. rate
     each branch at about 1.2–1.5× its base-case flow, rounded, and document the rule.
   - Use a load profile with a growth factor, so overloading appears at peak times while the generators' voltage
     regulators hold voltages. Optionally include one branch out of service as an N-1-like stress.

For each candidate, iterate on the parameters (profile type, factor, magnitude, PV placement, time window, resolution,
overrides) until the results are clearly interesting but still believable. Prefer a 1-week window at 15 or 60 min, so
it runs fast and plays well in the snapshot.
