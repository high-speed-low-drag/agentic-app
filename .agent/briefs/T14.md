## Context
`frontend/src/results/views/NetworkSnapshotView.tsx` has a time slider (`<input type="range">`), a "step X / N" label,
playback (`playback.ts`, from T11) and a legend at the bottom-left of the diagram. A timestep's timestamp comes from
`summary.timestamps_start` plus `t × resolution_minutes` (see `timestepTime`). The backend PV generator
(`backend/src/gridsim/profiles/pv.py`) uses declination `23.44° × sin(2π(284 + doy)/365)` and the hour angle
`15° × (hour − solar_noon_hour)`, with defaults latitude 52 and solar_noon_hour 13.5.

## Build
1. **Solar helper**, a new pure module, e.g. `frontend/src/lib/solar.ts`. Given a date, a latitude and a solar noon hour,
   it returns the sunrise and sunset clock hours, using the same formula as the backend PV generator (sun elevation 0).
   - In polar day or night (|tan(lat) × tan(decl)| ≥ 1), return all-day or no daylight.
   - Also provide `isDaylight(timestamp, lat, noon)`.
2. **Latitude and noon source:** the run's frozen case (`useRunContext` / `runCase`). Take `latitude` and
   `solar_noon_hour` from the first `pv` profile assignment's params. If there is no pv assignment, use the defaults
   52 / 13.5. Show the source in a tooltip, e.g. "Daylight from PV profile (lat 52°)" or "default latitude 52°".
3. **Clock overlay:** a `SnapshotClock` component, bottom-right inside the diagram, opposite the legend, non-interactive
   with `pointer-events: none`. It must not cover the zoom controls (top-right).
   - A 24-hour dial (about 96–112 px) with midnight at the top and labels 00, 06, 12, 18. Shade the arc from sunrise to
     sunset light (a warm, warning-ish token) and the night arc darker (a muted token), with a hand at the current time.
   - Next to it: the digital time "HH:MM" in mono, the weekday and date "Sun 5 Jul 2026", a badge saying "Daylight"
     (warm) or "Night" (muted), and small text "Sunrise 05:18 · sunset 21:54".
   - It updates every frame during playback and slider scrubbing.
   - Works in light and dark mode using theme tokens. Collapse it to the digital time only if the diagram is narrower than about 560 px.
4. **Daylight-shaded slider:** behind or under the range input, draw a track (SVG or CSS gradient segments) aligned with
   the slider's usable width, accounting for the thumb inset.
   - Light segments for daylight and dark for night, for every day in the run, with thin day dividers. Where there is
     room, weekday labels under the track (e.g. at least 28 px per day).
   - If the run spans more than about 31 days, draw no per-day bands. Draw month dividers and labels instead, with a
     subtle seasonal daylight-length shading (optional) or no shading.
   - Keep the native range input for accessibility and the keyboard.
5. Use theme tokens only, in both light and dark mode.
